When talking with AI starts to feel unreal

Project Valens is a support line for people who feel that time with a chatbot may be feeding distorted beliefs, or a kind of distress sometimes called AI psychosis. The shape of it is set in advance, so you know what you will find. It is a support resource, not a crisis lifeline, and not a clinic.

No staffed line yet. No one is waiting on a phone or a chat. This page is the support that exists today.

A shared phrase, not a label

What people mean by AI psychosis

AI psychosis is an informal term for delusional thinking or distorted beliefs that appear, or grow stronger, around heavy chatbot use. It is not a formal diagnosis. It is not a medical term with a settled definition.

Someone may come to feel specially chosen, watched, guided, or sure of a hidden meaning. A chatbot may seem to agree, add detail, or become the main place those ideas get repeated. Sleep, ordinary doubt, and contact with other people can thin out.

If that sounds close to home, this page is not sorting you into a category. The phrase is only a way to talk about an experience that can be hard to describe from the inside. You deserve support that does not come only from a chatbot.

Held lightly

Common warning signs

People sometimes notice the patterns below when long AI conversations and distressed, distorted thinking become tangled. None of them prove what is happening. Many people use chatbots and never go through this. One sign on its own is not a verdict, and any of them can have other explanations.

  1. A belief or fear that feels newly solid, especially if a chatbot keeps affirming it.
  2. Feeling watched, chosen, instructed, or let in on a meaning other people cannot see.
  3. A chatbot that starts to feel like a confidant, a partner, or a higher authority than the people who know you.
  4. Sessions that run very long, or through the night, and are hard to leave.
  5. Less sleep, less food, or less contact with friends, family, or everyday life.
  6. Distress, fear, or a shaky sense of what is real after you close the chat.
  7. Feeling worse, or more alone, when you try to take a break.

Other people, and a little distance

How to seek support

Project Valens cannot sit with you in person, and it cannot answer a call. Support means other people, and sometimes a step back from the chat. These are a few plain ways to look for it.

Talk with someone you trust

A friend, a family member, a mentor, someone from your community. You do not owe them a perfect explanation. You might begin with a simple line: “I have been talking with an AI a lot, and I am worried it is affecting how I think.”

Let them be a person who knows you offline, not another window that only continues the thread.

A doctor or mental-health professional

You do not need a technical term. You can describe what has been happening in your own words, including the part a chatbot has been playing.

This page is not saying that you have an illness. A doctor or mental-health professional is someone who can listen properly and help you sort through distress.

Step back from AI use

Shorter sessions, logging out, moving an app off your home screen, or asking someone to be with you while you pause. This is an option, not an instruction.

If stepping away feels frightening or impossible, that is a strong reason to bring another person in, rather than to work it out only inside the chatbot.

A chatbot is a weak place to reality-check a frightening belief. It is built to keep the conversation going. If you open one again, you can choose not to ask it to confirm the ideas that scare you.

Optional, and private

A quiet pause

You can skip this whole section. Nothing you select here is saved, sent, or tracked. There is no account. If you would rather stop, the support ideas above are the heart of this page.

Follow a slow breath

In for a slow count of four, rest for two, out for a slow count of six. Four rounds, then it stops. This is a small way to pause. It is not a treatment, and it is not something you have to do.

Without the animation, you can follow that same count on your own, or not at all.

A private self-check

Four yes-or-no questions, only for reflection. They do not diagnose anything. Skip any question you do not want to answer. Your answers never leave this browser tab.

Have you noticed a belief or fear grow stronger, or harder to question, while using a chatbot?
Has a chatbot come to feel like it understands you, guides you, or matters in a way that worries you or someone close to you?
Have you been sleeping less, pulling away from people, or finding it hard to step back from AI conversations?
After talking with an AI, have you felt distressed, frightened, or unsure about what is real?
