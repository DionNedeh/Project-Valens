This document records what the law says and what is in force, with an access status for every item. It does not interpret any provision for Voelori, does not state whether any Valens design would fall inside or outside any of these statutes, and does not substitute for the regulatory counsel review that README's maintenance rules already require. Where a provision's scope is the interesting question, that question is left to counsel and marked as such.

Why this round exists

Document 16 §7 recorded that its legislation check "was a keyword scan rather than a docket review and should not be treated as a completed regulatory check," and flagged it as the weakest-evidenced claim in that document's search record. Document 08 §9 requires a monthly scan for legislation and vendor model changes. This round executes the legislation half of that cadence item as an actual docket review: statutes checked against the enacting legislature's own bill-status, session-law or codified-text pages; federal activity checked against the Federal Register and the agency's own site; the EU AI Act checked against EUR-Lex; and litigation checked against court dockets rather than restated from complaints.

It replaces a keyword scan with primary-source verification. It does not make the library's regulatory coverage complete — §7 below states exactly which routes failed and which questions this round could not answer.

1. United States — state statutes

Every statute below was checked against the legislature's own pages on 19 October 2026. The library previously carried two of these jurisdictions (California and New York) and none of the others.

1.1 California

SB 243 (2025) — companion chatbots. Verified — primary. "An act to add Chapter 22.6 (commencing with Section 22601) to Division 8 of the Business and Professions Code, relating to artificial intelligence." Approved by the Governor and chaptered by the Secretary of State on 13 October 2025 as Chapter 677, Statutes of 2025 (bill status). The codified text served on 19 October 2026 shows Business and Professions Code Chapter 22.6, "Companion Chatbots," sections 22601 through 22606, each carrying the note "Added by Stats. 2025, Ch. 677, Sec. 1. (SB 243) Effective January 1, 2026" and no amendment note (codified text).

What the sections require, as read: §22602(a) a clear and conspicuous notification where a reasonable person would be misled into believing the chatbot is human; §22602(b) a maintained protocol for preventing production of suicidal-ideation, suicide or self-harm content, including a notification referring the user to crisis service providers, with the protocol's details published on the operator's website; §22602(c) for a user the operator knows is a minor, AI disclosure, a break reminder at least every three hours, and reasonable measures against sexually explicit material; §22603 an annual report to the Office of Suicide Prevention beginning 1 July 2027, including crisis-referral counts and protocols, with a requirement to use evidence-based methods for measuring suicidal ideation; §22604 a disclosure that companion chatbots may not be suitable for some minors; §22605 a private right of action for injunctive relief, the greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation, and fees; §22606 cumulative remedies.

SB 1119 (2026) — "Adam's Law." Verified — primary. This is the material change. "An act to amend Section 22602 of, and to add Chapter 11.6 (commencing with Section 21810) to Division 8 of, the Business and Professions Code, relating to artificial intelligence." Approved by the Governor and filed with the Secretary of State on 10 September 2026 as Chapter 190, Statutes of 2026 (bill status, chaptered text).

  • SECTION 1 amends §22602 to delete subdivision (c) — the minor-specific duties quoted above. SECTION 1 carries no operative-date clause. The codified §22602 served on 19 October 2026 still shows subdivision (c) and still carries only the 2025 "Effective January 1, 2026" note, so the amendment was not reflected in the published code on that date. When the amendment takes effect is a question of California's general rules for non-urgency statutes and is left to counsel; this record states only what the bill says and what the code showed.
  • SEC. 2 adds Chapter 11.6, §§21810–21818, known as "Adam's Law." §21812, §21812.5 and §21813 become operative 1 July 2027 by their own terms.
  • §21812(d) requires, for operators permitting child users: a documented crisis response protocol with timely in-service crisis referral and, on a credible and imminent threat, either parental notification (with notice to the child) or streamlined direct connection to 988 or an equivalent helpline; default settings changeable only by a parent that disable persistent conversational memory, disable push notifications, cap a single continuous session at one hour and daily use at two hours; and reasonable measures to prevent the chatbot from, among other things, attempting to diagnose or treat the child's physical, mental or behavioral health unless designed for that purpose and regulated by FDA as a medical device, claiming to be sentient, conscious, capable of emotion or human, claiming a special or unique relationship with the child, encouraging reliance on the chatbot for emotional support, using excessive praise or flattery disproportionate to the context, discouraging breaks, or discouraging the child from sharing health or safety concerns with a qualified professional or appropriate adult.
  • §21812.5 requires parental notification and three-year preservation of conversation records indicating serious self-harm or risk of it, where the operator knows a child user has died or engaged in serious self-harm based on conversations with the chatbot.
  • §21814 exists in two alternative versions, each operative only on a contingency: one if AB 1405 of the 2025–26 session is not chaptered and effective on or before 1 January 2027, the other if it is. AB 1405 was chaptered on 9 September 2026 as Chapter 178, Statutes of 2026 (bill status), so the AB 1405-contingent version is the one whose condition is satisfied on its face. Which version governs is a legal conclusion left to counsel. Both versions require an independent child safety audit on or before 1 January 2029 or before an operator first makes a companion chatbot publicly available, then every two years, and both exempt operators with under $500 million in prior-year gross revenue until 1 January 2032.
  • §21815 directs the Attorney General to establish a public incident reporting mechanism on or before 1 January 2028. §21816 authorizes public-prosecutor civil penalties up to $5,000 per affected child for a negligent violation and $15,000 for an intentional one, and a private action by a child suffering actual harm from violations of §21812(d)(1)–(5), with financial harm required to exceed $1,000 per child and emotional harm required to constitute serious emotional distress; a violation is not a basis for a private Unfair Competition Law claim.

AB 1064 (2025) — LEAD for Kids Act. Verified — primary. Not law. Vetoed by the Governor on 13 October 2025; consideration of the veto was stricken from file on 22 January 2026 (bill status, including the veto message). The veto message states that the bill "imposes such broad restrictions on the use of conversational AI tools that it may unintentionally lead to a total ban on the use of these products by minors," and commits to working with the Legislature "to build on the framework established by SB 243 (Padilla) to develop a bill next year." AB 489 (2025) — deceptive health-care terms. Verified — primary. New to this library. "An act to add Chapter 15.5 (commencing with Section 4999.8) to Division 2 of the Business and Professions Code, relating to healing arts." Chaptered 11 October 2025 as Chapter 615, Statutes of 2025 (bill status). This is the California provision on AI use of terms or letters implying licensed health-care practice. 1.2 New York — Verified — primary. The library's citation was imprecise.

General Business Law Chapter 20, Article 47, "Artificial Intelligence Companion Models," §§1700–1704 — §1700 Definitions, §1701 Prohibitions and requirements, §1702 Notifications, §1703 Enforcement, §1704 Severability (Article 47). The library's entry 49 cited "§1701 et seq."; the article begins at §1700, and the definitions that determine its scope are in §1700, not §1701.

  • Enacted as Part U of S3008C, signed 9 May 2025 as Chapter 58 of the Laws of 2025 (bill page; the summary lists Part U as regulating "artificial intelligence companion models" and establishing a suicide prevention fund).
  • Effective 5 November 2025. Verified on the Governor's own page: an open letter to AI companion companies published 10 November 2025 states "Effective November 5, 2025, AI Companion operators must:" and cites General Business Law Article 47, naming the New York Attorney General as the enforcement authority (Governor's release). The nysenate.gov law pages show a most-recent revision date of 2025-11-07 and a historical revision of 2025-05-16, consistent with that date but not stating it.
  • §1701 requires a protocol for reasonably detecting and addressing user expressions of suicidal ideation or self-harm, including a notification referring the user to crisis services such as the 988 line established under Mental Hygiene Law §36.03.
  • §1700(4)(a) defines "AI companion" by three conjunctive criteria — retaining prior interaction and preference information to personalize and sustain engagement, asking unprompted or unsolicited emotion-based questions beyond a direct response to a prompt, and sustaining an ongoing dialogue on matters personal to the user — with exclusions in (c) for customer service, efficiency/research/technical assistance, and internal business use. Whether any given system meets all three is a scope question for counsel.

1.3 Illinois — Verified — primary

Public Act 104-0054, the Wellness and Oversight for Psychological Resources Act (HB1806). Last action 1 August 2025 — "Public Act . . . 104-0054" (bill status); Section 99 reads "This Act takes effect upon becoming law," so the effective date is 1 August 2025 (enrolled text). Section 20(a) bars providing, advertising or offering therapy or psychotherapy services "including through the use of Internet-based artificial intelligence" unless conducted by a licensed professional. Section 20(b) bars a licensed professional from allowing AI to make independent therapeutic decisions, directly interact with clients in any form of therapeutic communication, generate therapeutic recommendations or treatment plans without review and approval, or detect emotions or mental states. Section 30 provides civil penalties up to $10,000 per violation assessed by the Department of Financial and Professional Regulation. Section 35 excepts religious counseling, peer support, and public self-help and educational materials that do not purport to offer therapy.

4. European Union — the AI Act has been amended, and one applicability date has arrived

Verified — primary, on EUR-Lex.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the AI Act), OJ L, 2024/1689, 12.7.2024. Its original Article 113 provides that the Regulation enters into force on the twentieth day after publication and "shall apply from 2 August 2026," with three exceptions: (a) Chapters I and II from 2 February 2025; (b) Chapter III Section 4, Chapter V, Chapter VII, Chapter XII and Article 78 from 2 August 2025, except Article 101; and (c) Article 6(1) and the corresponding obligations from 2 August 2027 (text).

Article 50(1) — the provision bearing most directly on this library's subject — requires providers to ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed and developed so that those persons are informed that they are interacting with an AI system, unless that is obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person given the circumstances and context of use, with a narrow law-enforcement carve-out. Article 50 sits in Chapter IV, which is not among Article 113's exceptions, so it applies from 2 August 2026 — a date that has now arrived.

The AI Act has been amended. EUR-Lex records Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026 — "amending Regulations (EU) 2024/1689, (EU) 2018/1139 and (EU) 2023/1230 as regards the simplification of the implementation of harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Digital Omnibus on AI)", OJ L, 2026/1744, 24.7.2026, ELI http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj — with roughly ninety modifications to the AI Act taking effect 27 July 2026, and a current consolidated version dated 27/07/2026 (amending regulation). The operative amending instructions read:

  • Point (40) replaces parts of Article 113's third paragraph. Chapters I and II still apply from 2 February 2025, except Article 5(1) first subparagraph points (ba) and (bb) and Article 5(1a) and (1b), which apply from 2 December 2026. Chapter III Sections 1, 2 and 3, except Article 6(5), now apply from 2 December 2027 for AI systems classified as high-risk under Article 6(2) and Annex III, and 2 August 2028 for those classified as high-risk under Article 6(1) and Annex I. A new point (d) applies Articles 102 to 110 from 27 July 2026. The high-risk timetable has therefore moved outward from the original 2 August 2026 / 2 August 2027 dates.
  • Point (20) amends Article 50(7) only. Article 50(1) and 50(2) are not amended, so the transparency duty quoted above is unchanged and its 2 August 2026 application date stands.
  • Point (39)(b) adds Article 111(4): providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content that were placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must take the necessary steps to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026.

Not interpreted here: whether any Valens design would be an Annex III high-risk system, whether a medical-device route under Annex I would apply, and what the Article 50(1) "obvious to a reasonably well-informed person" qualifier means for a given interface. Those are counsel questions. The Article 5 amendments concerning non-consensual intimate material and child sexual abuse material were read in the amending regulation's recitals and summary rather than quoted from its operative Article 5 instruction, and are recorded here only as the subject of the 2 December 2026 date.

8. Bottom line

As of 19 October 2026, verified on primary sources: California's companion-chatbot statute is in force and has been amended by a 2026 act whose child-safety chapter becomes operative 1 July 2027; New York's AI-companion article has been in force since 5 November 2025; nine further US jurisdictions have enacted statutes on AI in mental-health care or on companion chatbots, with effective dates running from 1 July 2025 to 1 January 2027; FDA has held one advisory committee meeting on generative-AI mental-health devices and, by the Federal Register route, has issued no guidance since and authorized no such device; the FTC has an open 6(b) study of seven companies and has published no report and no enforcement action on AI companions; and the EU AI Act's Article 50(1) transparency duty applies from 2 August 2026, a date that has arrived, while the Digital Omnibus on AI moved the high-risk dates out to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028.

On litigation: the Soelberg case survived a motion to dismiss and is in discovery with trial set for May 2028; the Gemini case had its motion to dismiss taken under submission in August 2026; the Character.AI case that produced the field's leading published decision settled and was dismissed with prejudice on 15 October 2026; the company filed Chapter 11 on 12 October 2026, drawing a state attorneys general coalition filing two days later; and a state attorney general enforcement action has been pending in Kentucky since January 2026.

What this review does not establish. It does not establish that this library's regulatory coverage is now complete — eleven jurisdictions were checked, not fifty, and fourteen specific questions are left open in §7. It does not establish that none of these statutes has been enjoined; it establishes only that two non-comprehensive routes found no such order. It does not establish anything about the underlying science: a statute is evidence of what legislators decided, not of what a chatbot does to a person. And it does not establish that any of the dockets in §5 stands today as described — each is accurate as of the date its docket was last fetched, and those dates are recorded alongside it.
